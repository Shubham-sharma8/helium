Helium Help Center

Combine multiple Google Drive accounts into a single fast macOS virtual disk at /Volumes/Helium.

Placement Engine

Storage Pool Placement Engine: Both vs Mode 1 vs Mode 2

  • Both (Smart Whole-File + Auto Striping) — Recommended: Keeps everyday files (< threshold or fitting on one drive) as a single intact file in Google Drive so you can preview and open them directly on drive.google.com, and automatically switches to cross-account segment striping when a file is larger than any single account's free space.
  • Mode 1: Whole-File Smart Placement: Always keeps every file as 1 complete, unsplit file on your highest-priority Google Drive account that has enough free space. Best when you want 100% of files directly playable and downloadable from Google Drive web without Helium.
  • Mode 2: Always Cross-Account Striping: Splits every uploaded file into chunked segments (64 MiB–256 MiB) distributed across all linked Google Drives in parallel. Maximizes combined bandwidth and pools small fragments of free space across accounts.
Upload Routing

Google Account Upload Priority (#1 → #2 → #3)

  • Priority Order (#1 → #2 → #3…): Uploads go to your #1 Google Drive first until it fills up, then automatically overflow to #2, #3, and beyond. Reorder accounts anytime in the Google Drive Pool tab.
  • Most Free Space: Automatically selects whichever linked Google Drive currently has the most free gigabytes.
  • Preferred Target Account: Pins a specific Google Drive for your next uploads, with automatic overflow if full.
  • Moving Existing Files: Right-click any file in Helium Home (or open its Inspector) and choose Move to Google Account to migrate it to another linked Google Drive.
Finder & Privacy

Finder Virtual Disk (/Volumes/Helium) & Zero-Knowledge Encryption

  • Zero Local Disk Space: Files in /Volumes/Helium stream on demand and take 0 bytes on your Mac until opened. Click the Pin button on any file to keep it available offline.
  • Zero-Knowledge Encryption: Enable AES-256-GCM encryption in Settings → Privacy to encrypt file contents and filenames before they leave your Mac. Save your 52-character Recovery Key in Apple Passwords.
